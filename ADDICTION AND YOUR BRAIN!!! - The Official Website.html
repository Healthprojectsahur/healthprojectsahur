♠ GAMBLING AFFECTS ON MENTAL HEALTH ♠

Gambling is the only behavior that psychiatry officially classifies as an addiction the same way it classifies drugs. In the DSM-5, the manual doctors use to diagnose, gambling disorder sits in the same chapter as substance use disorders because it hijacks the same dopamine reward pathways. Your brain does not know the difference between a slot machine and a chemical.

► WHAT THE RESEARCH SAYS

  • Problem gambling travels in a pack. Research consistently shows gambling problems occur alongside nicotine dependence, alcohol use disorders, depression, and anxiety, and the relationship runs both directions.
  • Depression risk multiplies. Problem gamblers were around three times more likely than non-gamblers to suffer a major depressive episode.
  • Two out of three.
  • The crisis risk is real. <5% of problem gamblers reported a suicide attempt in the past year, compared with 0.6% of people showing no sign of problem gambling, and researchers argue problem gamblers should be treated as a high-risk group in suicide prevention.

► WHY IT HITS TEENAGERS SO HARD

The reward part of the adolescent brain develops faster than the prefrontal cortex, which is the part that handles impulse control and long-term planning. That gap is why a 16 year old can fully understand that parlays lose money and chase one anyway. Sportsbooks are built on near misses, and a near miss lights up the brain almost like a win, which is what keeps people pulling the lever.

⚑ IF THIS IS HITTING TOO CLOSE TO HOME ⚑
Call or text 988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, or call 1-800-GAMBLER for the National Problem Gambling Helpline. Both are free, open 24/7, and talking to a school counselor counts too. Nobody has ever gotten in trouble for asking for help.

⚽ MENTAL HEALTH IN SPORTS ⚽

Athletes get treated like they are made of iron, and the numbers say otherwise. Three pressures stack on top of each other: performance anxiety, identity built entirely around a sport that can end with one injury, and a locker room culture where admitting you are struggling reads as weakness. Add legalized sports betting and now strangers have money riding on a teenager's free throws.

► THE BETTING PROBLEM IS NOW A MENTAL HEALTH PROBLEM

  • <46% of men's basketball players in the NCAA's 2026 Student-Athlete Needs, Aspirations and Perspectives survey reported experiencing abuse over betting losses, the highest share among men's sports.
  • <36% of Division I men's basketball athletes reported social media abuse related to sports betting within the last year, and 29% said a student on campus had told them they bet on their team.

► THE DOUBLE WHAMMY

An athlete can be battling an addiction of their own while also absorbing harassment from strangers who are battling theirs. One study of Division I athletes found online harassment correlated positively with depression. The kid who missed the shot goes home and reads 40 messages about it, and that is now a standard Tuesday.

⚠ 4 ADDICTIONS THAT AFFECT MENTAL HEALTH ⚠

1. SMOKING & VAPING 🚬

Nicotine sells itself as a stress reliever and then manufactures the stress it claims to fix. Withdrawal between hits causes anxiety, irritability, and low mood, so the relief you feel is your own withdrawal ending.

THE NUMBERS:

FOR ATHLETES: nicotine constricts blood vessels and wrecks sleep quality, and sleep is where the body actually repairs itself. Worse recovery, worse practice, worse mood.

2. PHONE & SOCIAL MEDIA 📱

Phone addiction is not an official DSM diagnosis, and that honesty matters for our project. What research does show is a strong link between heavy social media use and worse teen mental health, which is why the US Surgeon General has issued advisories about it twice since 2021.

THE NUMBERS: <21% of adolescents ages 12 to 17 reported anxiety symptoms in the past two weeks and 17% reported depression symptoms.

FOR ATHLETES: the phone is where the harassment arrives. See the sports section above.

3. DRINKING 🍺

Alcohol is a depressant, full stop. It feels like it turns off anxiety because it suppresses the central nervous system, then the rebound the next day leaves anxiety higher than where it started. It also destroys REM sleep, which is the stage that processes emotion.

WHY TEENS SPECIFICALLY: the brain keeps developing into the mid-twenties, and alcohol interferes with that construction project. Depression and alcohol misuse also feed each other, so treating only one tends to fail.

FOR ATHLETES: dehydration, lost sleep, slower reaction time, and longer injury recovery. One night out can cost most of a week of training.

4. GAMBLING 🎰

The one with an official diagnosis and a betting app on nearly every phone in the building. See the full section above for the research, but the short version is that it reliably brings depression and anxiety with it, and it is the only item on this list you can lose your rent to in 15 seconds.

☀ GOOD WAYS TO COPE ☀

THE OFFICIAL COPING WHEEL™ (it does nothing, it just spins)

Every addiction on this page is a shortcut to feeling better, which means the only thing that beats it is a real route to feeling better. These are the strategies that actually hold up in research.

  1. Tell one person out loud. A friend, a parent, a coach, a counselor. Addiction depends on secrecy, and saying it to one human being takes most of its power away.
  2. Protect your sleep before anything else. Seven to nine hours, phone out of arm's reach. Sleep deprivation imitates anxiety and depression so closely that fixing sleep first makes everything else easier to read.
  3. Move your body on purpose. Exercise is one of the most reliably supported mood interventions there is, and you do not need a gym for it. A walk counts.
  4. Learn box breathing. Inhale four counts, hold four, exhale four, hold four. Repeat five times. It physically slows your heart rate, and it works in a hallway, on a bench, or in a bathroom before a test.
  5. Put friction between you and the habit. Delete the app, leave the card at home, set a screen curfew, keep the vape somewhere inconvenient. Willpower loses to design, so change the design.
  6. Replace, do not just remove. The habit was doing a job for you, whether that was boredom, stress, or social connection. Pick something that does the same job: lifting, music, drawing, cooking, a sport, a group chat that is not about betting.
  7. Write it down. Journaling what triggered the urge and what happened after builds the pattern recognition you need to see it coming next time.
  8. Use the free professional help. School counselors, 988 for crisis support, 1-800-GAMBLER for gambling, and free quit-vaping text programs all exist and cost nothing.

ASKING FOR HELP IS THE STRONGEST THING ON THIS LIST.
